=== 2. where the pole is ===
   Theta(t) = theta_alpha(t) * S2(t)  ~  sqrt(pi/t) * [1/t + 1/3 + t/15 + ...]
            =  sqrt(pi) [ t^-3/2 + (1/3) t^-1/2 + (1/15) t^+1/2 + ... ]

   Mellin:  INT_0^lam t^(s-1) t^((k-3)/2) dt  has a pole at s = -(k-3)/2
     k=0  ->  pole at s = +3/2
     k=2  ->  pole at s = +1/2
     k=4  ->  pole at s = -1/2      <-- EXACTLY where we work

   coefficient of t^+1/2 is sqrt(pi)/15 = 0.1181635901 != 0
   -> zeta(s) HAS A POLE at s = -1/2 on S^1 x S^2.
   On a FLAT torus the expansion terminates at k=0 (no curvature terms),
   so there is no pole and Z(-1/2) is finite and unambiguous.

=== 3. but the DIFFERENCE is always finite ===
   theta_P(t) - theta_A(t) = sqrt(pi/t) * 4 * sum_{k>=1 odd} exp(-pi^2 k^2 / t)
   t=   0.5  thP-thA = 2.682381008e-8   predicted 2.682381008e-8
   t=   0.2  thP-thA = 5.868898145e-21   predicted 5.86875906e-21
   t=   0.1  thP-thA = 2.067951531e-25   predicted 3.072469837e-42
   t=  0.05  thP-thA =            0.0   predicted 5.954637475e-85

   exponentially small as t->0, for ANY manifold factor.
   So Theta_P - Theta_A is exponentially small, the poles CANCEL,
   and Z_A - Z_P is finite even when each term diverges.
