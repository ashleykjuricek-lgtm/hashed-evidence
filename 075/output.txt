TEST 1 -- would SEMANTIC HASHING have caught the Tier-2 errors?
==========================================================================

  sealed : R sits a hair under 1/24
  correct: R sits a hair over 1/24
  char similarity 0.894   token overlap 0.714

  sealed : 79.0% of shells below the cutoff are empty
  correct: 76% of shells below the cutoff are empty
  char similarity 0.951   token overlap 0.778

  sealed : the two divisions are complementary, each blind exactly where the other sees
  correct: the two divisions are independent, neither lens predicts the other
  char similarity 0.648   token overlap 0.333

  A semantic hash is DESIGNED so that small changes in meaning give small
  changes in code. 'under' vs 'over' is one token in eight -- these collide
  by construction. Semantic hashing is not merely unhelpful for Tier 2;
  it is ACTIVELY BLIND to it. That is the design goal, not a defect.

TEST 2 -- would a MORE PRECISE LANGUAGE have caught them?
==========================================================================
  The Tier-2 failure was not a shortage of expressive precision.
  'under' and 'over' are unambiguous in English. So are adhah and upari.
  The failure was not CHECKING the sentence against the table beside it.

  A precisely-worded wrong sentence is still wrong. Test:
     English  : 'R sits a hair under 1/24'   -- unambiguous, and false
     Any lang : same proposition, same falsity
  -> a denser medium changes what CAN be said, not what IS checked.

  And 074 tested what DOES catch them: rendering the direction-word
  from a predicate over the witness output.  3 of 3.  Language-independent.

TEST 3 -- would a LEARNED POLICY have caught them? What is the training set?
==========================================================================
  total recorded errors: 17
     Tier1      11
     Tier2       3
     retrieval   1
     identity    1
     process     1

  A Q-network needs (state, action, reward) trajectories. We have 17 errors,
  each resolved ONCE, with no repeated episodes and no counterfactuals.
  That is not a training set; it is an anecdote list.

  And for every case, the fix that worked was a FIXED RULE, not a policy:
     floor at ten               -> re-ran as a ratio
     PSLQ at 24 digits          -> stated the digit budget
     tolerance vs truncation    -> widened the truncation
     11 lobes on 10-fold        -> 11 cannot divide 10
     truncated theta 4297       -> the number was absurd
     RP^3 integration v1        -> the cut WAS the answer
     RP^3 integration v2        -> lambda-invariance test
     18 sigma                   -> writing the repro spec

  None of these was a wrong ACTION CHOSEN FROM A MENU. They were:
     (a) a test not run,  (b) a table not read,  (c) a frame not questioned.
  A policy over {run witness, fetch gloss, retrieve, escalate} addresses none.
